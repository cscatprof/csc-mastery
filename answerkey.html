Sentence Structure — Questions 1–50

Question 1 C

Correct answer: She doesn't know the answer.

Explanation: “Doesn't” already carries the present-tense marker, so the main verb stays in its base form, “know.”

Quick lesson: After does/doesn't, use the base verb: doesn't know, doesn't go.

Question 2 B

Correct answer: The students were preparing for the examination.

Explanation: “Students” is plural, so the past form of “be” is “were.”

Quick lesson: Use was with singular subjects and were with plural subjects.

Question 3 A

Correct answer: Because it was raining, the event was postponed.

Explanation: “Because” already introduces the reason. Adding “so” creates an unnecessary double connector.

Quick lesson: Use either “Because ..., ...” or “..., so ...”; not both together.

Question 4 D

Correct answer: He is interested in learning new skills.

Explanation: The correct expression is “interested in,” followed by a noun or an -ing verb.

Quick lesson: Remember: interested in learning, interested in music.

Question 5 B

Correct answer: Neither the teacher nor the students were ready.

Explanation: With “neither ... nor,” the verb commonly agrees with the nearer subject. “Students” is plural.

Quick lesson: When subjects are joined by neither/nor, check the subject closest to the verb.

Question 6 A

Correct answer: She enjoys reading, writing, and painting.

Explanation: All three activities use the same -ing form, making the list parallel.

Quick lesson: Keep items in a list in the same grammatical form.

Question 7 C

Correct answer: The manager, along with his employees, is attending the meeting.

Explanation: The main subject is “manager.” The phrase “along with his employees” does not make it plural.

Quick lesson: Phrases such as along with, as well as, and together with do not change the main subject.

Question 8 B

Correct answer: I do not know where the office is.

Explanation: An indirect question uses normal statement word order: subject before verb.

Quick lesson: Direct: Where is the office? Indirect: I know where the office is.

Question 9 A

Correct answer: Every applicant must submit their form before Friday.

Explanation: “Every applicant” is singular, and singular “they/their” is accepted in modern standard English. “Must” takes the base verb.

Quick lesson: Use must + base verb. Singular they/their is acceptable when gender is unknown.

Question 10 A

Correct answer: The report was completed by the employee yesterday.

Explanation: “Report” is singular, so the passive construction uses “was completed.”

Quick lesson: Past passive: was/were + past participle.

Question 11 C

Correct answer: Although he was tired, he continued working.

Explanation: “Although” already shows contrast, so “but” or “so” is not needed.

Quick lesson: Use although + clause without adding but in the same construction.

Question 12 B

Correct answer: The teacher explained the lesson to us.

Explanation: The standard pattern is “explain something to someone.”

Quick lesson: Say “explain the lesson to us,” not “explain us the lesson.”

Question 13 D

Correct answer: My brother is taller than I am.

Explanation: “Taller” is already the comparative form. “More taller” and “most taller” are incorrect double comparisons.

Quick lesson: Use taller, not more taller.

Question 14 A

Correct answer: The employees who work hard usually achieve their goals.

Explanation: “Who” refers to the plural noun “employees,” so the verb is “work.”

Quick lesson: A relative-clause verb agrees with the noun that “who” refers to.

Question 15 C

Correct answer: She has gone to the office.

Explanation: The present perfect uses has/have + past participle. The past participle of “go” is “gone.”

Quick lesson: Present perfect: has/have + past participle.

Question 16 B

Correct answer: The reason for his absence was that he was sick.

Explanation: This is a clear, grammatically correct way to state the reason without the awkward “reason ... is because” construction.

Quick lesson: Use “The reason was that ...” or “The reason for ... was that ...”.

Question 17 C

Correct answer: If I were you, I would accept the offer.

Explanation: “Were” is the standard subjunctive form used in this unreal or hypothetical expression.

Quick lesson: For advice in a hypothetical situation, use “If I were you ...”.

Question 18 A

Correct answer: The office needs employees who are honest, responsible, and hardworking.

Explanation: The three words are all adjectives, so the list is parallel.

Quick lesson: Use the same part of speech for items in a series.

Question 19 D

Correct answer: He asked me whether I was ready.

Explanation: An indirect yes/no question uses “whether” or “if” followed by statement word order.

Quick lesson: Use whether/if + subject + verb in an indirect question.

Question 20 B

Correct answer: The book, together with the notebooks, belongs to her.

Explanation: The main subject is the singular “book.” “Together with the notebooks” is extra information.

Quick lesson: Ignore interrupting phrases when deciding subject-verb agreement.

Question 21 A

Correct answer: She speaks English more fluently than her brother.

Explanation: “Fluently” is an adverb describing how she speaks. The comparative form is “more fluently.”

Quick lesson: Use an adverb to describe a verb: speaks fluently.

Question 22 C

Correct answer: Before the meeting started, everyone had taken their seats.

Explanation: The past perfect uses “had” plus the past participle “taken.”

Quick lesson: Past perfect: had + past participle.

Question 23 B

Correct answer: The supervisor told the workers to complete the task.

Explanation: The pattern is “tell + person + to + base verb.”

Quick lesson: Use told someone to do something.

Question 24 D

Correct answer: There are fewer applicants this year than last year.

Explanation: “Applicants” is a countable plural noun, so “fewer” is the correct choice.

Quick lesson: Use fewer for countable things and less for uncountable amounts.

Question 25 B

Correct answer: Despite the heavy rain, the workers continued their duties.

Explanation: “Despite” is followed by a noun phrase, not “of” or a complete clause.

Quick lesson: Use despite + noun/-ing phrase; use although + subject + verb.

Question 26 B

Correct answer: The students have finished their assignments.

Explanation: “Students” is plural, so we use “have,” not “has.”

Quick lesson: Use have with plural subjects and has with singular subjects.

Question 27 C

Correct answer: She is good at solving mathematical problems.

Explanation: The correct expression is “good at” followed by a noun or an -ing verb.

Quick lesson: Remember: good at singing, writing, or solving problems.

Question 28 A

Correct answer: The manager gave the employees clear instructions.

Explanation: This follows the correct structure: subject + verb + indirect object + direct object.

Quick lesson: “Give someone something” is a correct sentence pattern.

Question 29 A

Correct answer: He works hard because he wants to succeed.

Explanation: “Because” introduces a complete clause. “Because of” is followed by a noun or noun phrase.

Quick lesson: Use because + subject + verb; use because of + noun.

Question 30 B

Correct answer: The teacher asked whether the students understood the lesson.

Explanation: Indirect questions use normal statement word order. We do not put “did” before the subject.

Quick lesson: Direct: Did they understand? Indirect: whether they understood.

Question 31 C

Correct answer: Neither of the answers is correct.

Explanation: In formal standard English, “neither” is treated as singular, so it takes “is.”

Quick lesson: For formal exam grammar, use neither + singular verb.

Question 32 C

Correct answer: She prefers coffee to tea.

Explanation: The standard expression is “prefer X to Y.”

Quick lesson: Use prefer + noun + to + noun.

Question 33 A

Correct answer: The students were excited about joining the competition.

Explanation: “Students” is plural, so we use “were.” “Excited about” is followed by an -ing verb.

Quick lesson: Use excited about doing something.

Question 34 C

Correct answer: He is the smartest student in the class.

Explanation: “Smartest” is already the superlative form. Adding “most” is unnecessary.

Quick lesson: Do not use most with adjectives that already end in -est.

Question 35 A

Correct answer: The officer explained the procedure to the applicants.

Explanation: The correct structure is “explain something to someone.”

Quick lesson: Say “explain the rules to us,” not “explain us the rules.”

Question 36 A

Correct answer: If she studies regularly, she will improve her scores.

Explanation: This is the first conditional: present simple in the if-clause and “will” in the result clause.

Quick lesson: First conditional: If + present simple, will + base verb.

Question 37 A

Correct answer: The documents were carefully checked by the clerk.

Explanation: “Documents” is plural, so the passive sentence uses “were.” “Carefully” describes how they were checked.

Quick lesson: Passive voice: subject + be verb + past participle.

Question 38 B

Correct answer: He is responsible for preparing the report.

Explanation: The correct expression is “responsible for,” followed by a noun or an -ing verb.

Quick lesson: Remember: responsible for doing something.

Question 39 C

Correct answer: The woman who works here is my aunt.

Explanation: “Who” refers to a person and acts as the subject of “works.”

Quick lesson: Use who for people, which for things, and whose for possession.

Question 40 B

Correct answer: The manager said the office would close early.

Explanation: “Said” can introduce a statement without naming the listener. “Told” usually needs an object, such as “told us.”

Quick lesson: Say something; tell someone something.

Question 41 B

Correct answer: He has been working here for five years.

Explanation: “For five years” describes a period of time. We use “for,” not “since,” for durations.

Quick lesson: Use for with a length of time and since with a starting point.

Question 42 B

Correct answer: The students not only studied hard but also practiced daily.

Explanation: The two parts are parallel: “studied” and “practiced” are both past-tense verbs.

Quick lesson: Keep the grammatical structure balanced after not only ... but also.

Question 43 C

Correct answer: The number of applicants is increasing every year.

Explanation: The subject is “the number,” which is singular. “Applicants” does not control the verb.

Quick lesson: Use the number of + plural noun + singular verb.

Question 44 A

Correct answer: She asked me where I was going.

Explanation: Indirect questions use statement word order: subject first, followed by the verb.

Quick lesson: Say “where I was going,” not “where was I going,” in an indirect question.

Question 45 B

Correct answer: The children enjoyed playing in the park.

Explanation: The verb “enjoy” is followed by a gerund, or an -ing verb.

Quick lesson: Use enjoy + verb-ing, such as enjoy reading or enjoy swimming.

Question 46 C

Correct answer: Despite being tired, he continued working.

Explanation: “Despite” can be followed by a noun or an -ing phrase. It is not followed directly by a complete clause.

Quick lesson: Use despite + noun/gerund; use although + subject + verb.

Question 47 D

Correct answer: The employees must follow the company rules.

Explanation: Modal verbs like “must” are followed by the base form of the verb.

Quick lesson: Use must + base verb: must go, must study, must follow.

Question 48 A

Correct answer: The book is too expensive for me to buy.

Explanation: This uses the correct pattern “too + adjective + for someone + to + verb.”

Quick lesson: Remember: too difficult to understand; too expensive to buy.

Question 49 B

Correct answer: She is one of the students who work hard.

Explanation: “Who” refers to “students,” which is plural. Therefore, the verb is “work.”

Quick lesson: In “one of the people who ...,” the verb after who normally agrees with people.

Question 50 C

Correct answer: The meeting was cancelled because the manager was absent.

Explanation: “Because” is followed by a complete clause: “the manager was absent.” “Because of” must be followed by a noun phrase.

Quick lesson: Use because + subject + verb; use because of + noun.

Sentence Structure — Questions 51–75

Question 51 B

Correct answer: The applicants have completed their registration.

Explanation: “Applicants” is plural, so the present perfect uses “have,” not “has.”

Quick lesson: Use have with plural subjects and has with singular subjects.

Question 52 C

Correct answer: She has been working at the company since 2020.

Explanation: “Since 2020” gives the starting point of an action that continues to the present. The present perfect continuous fits this meaning.

Quick lesson: Use since + starting point; use for + duration.

Question 53 A

Correct answer: The teacher encouraged the students to study harder.

Explanation: The correct pattern is “encourage someone to do something.”

Quick lesson: Use encourage + person + to + base verb.

Question 54 D

Correct answer: He is taller than his brother.

Explanation: “Taller” is already the comparative form, and comparisons use “than.”

Quick lesson: Use adjective + -er + than, or more + adjective + than.

Question 55 B

Correct answer: The report, along with the attachments, was submitted yesterday.

Explanation: The main subject is the singular “report.” The phrase “along with the attachments” does not change the subject.

Quick lesson: Ignore phrases such as along with when choosing the verb.

Question 56 C

Correct answer: I look forward to meeting you next week.

Explanation: In “look forward to,” the word “to” is a preposition, so it is followed by a noun or gerund.

Quick lesson: Use look forward to + verb-ing.

Question 57 A

Correct answer: The students were asked to bring their notebooks and pencils.

Explanation: “Students” is plural, so the passive form uses “were.” The structure “asked to bring” is correct.

Quick lesson: Passive: were + past participle; asked + to + base verb.

Question 58 D

Correct answer: He did not know the answer.

Explanation: After “did not,” use the base form “know,” not the past form “knew.”

Quick lesson: Use did not + base verb.

Question 59 B

Correct answer: The officer explained the rules clearly to the visitors.

Explanation: “Clearly” is an adverb describing how the officer explained the rules.

Quick lesson: Use an adverb to describe an action: explain clearly.

Question 60 C

Correct answer: The students, who were late, missed the instructions.

Explanation: “Who” refers to “students,” a plural noun, so the verb is “were.”

Quick lesson: Use who for people; make the relative-clause verb agree with its antecedent.

Question 61 A

Correct answer: She is responsible for organizing the event.

Explanation: The correct expression is “responsible for,” followed by a noun or gerund.

Quick lesson: Use responsible for + noun/verb-ing.

Question 62 A

Correct answer: The manager asked me why I was late.

Explanation: This is an indirect question, so it uses statement word order: subject before verb.

Quick lesson: Indirect question: question word + subject + verb.

Question 63 C

Correct answer: The committee has reached its decision.

Explanation: The sentence treats “committee” as one body and uses the singular possessive “its,” so “has” is the matching verb.

Quick lesson: Collective nouns can be singular when the group acts as one unit.

Question 64 B

Correct answer: The child was so tired that he could not walk.

Explanation: The correct result pattern is “so + adjective + that + clause.”

Quick lesson: Use so + adjective + that; use such + noun phrase + that.

Question 65 C

Correct answer: Neither the manager nor the employees are available.

Explanation: With “neither ... nor,” the verb commonly agrees with the nearer subject. “Employees” is plural.

Quick lesson: Check the subject nearest the verb in neither/nor constructions.

Question 66 A

Correct answer: She asked me if I could help her.

Explanation: An indirect yes/no question uses “if” followed by statement word order.

Quick lesson: Use if/whether + subject + verb in indirect questions.

Question 67 C

Correct answer: The students enjoy participating in school activities.

Explanation: “Students” is plural, so use “enjoy.” The verb “enjoy” is followed by a gerund.

Quick lesson: Use enjoy + verb-ing.

Question 68 A

Correct answer: The new employee is more careful than his coworkers.

Explanation: “Careful” is a longer adjective, so its comparative form is “more careful.”

Quick lesson: Use more + adjective + than for many adjectives with two or more syllables.

Question 69 A

Correct answer: The documents must be submitted before Friday.

Explanation: A passive construction after the modal “must” uses “be” plus the past participle “submitted.”

Quick lesson: Modal passive: must + be + past participle.

Question 70 C

Correct answer: The teacher suggested that he study every day.

Explanation: In formal standard English, “suggested that” can take the subjunctive base form “study.”

Quick lesson: After suggest that, formal exam English often uses the base verb: study, go, be.

Question 71 B

Correct answer: She speaks English as well as her sister.

Explanation: “Well” is the adverb needed to describe how she speaks. “As ... as” makes the comparison.

Quick lesson: Use as + adverb/adjective + as.

Question 72 C

Correct answer: The number of students has increased this year.

Explanation: The subject is “the number,” which is singular, so it takes “has.”

Quick lesson: Use the number of + plural noun + singular verb.

Question 73 A

Correct answer: He was absent because he was sick.

Explanation: “Because” is followed by a complete clause containing a subject and verb.

Quick lesson: Use because + subject + verb; use because of + noun phrase.

Question 74 D

Correct answer: The woman who called you is my supervisor.

Explanation: “Who” is the subject relative pronoun for a person performing the action “called.”

Quick lesson: Use who as the subject for people; whom as an object.

Question 75 B

Correct answer: After finishing the examination, the candidates left the room.

Explanation: After a preposition such as “after,” use a noun or gerund phrase. “Finishing” is the correct gerund.

Quick lesson: Use after + verb-ing when the same person performs both actions.
