CSC Mastery Answer Key

Fundamental Human Rights · Questions 1–25

Study tip: Read each explanation, then remember the key rule in its Quick Lesson.
QUESTION 1ANSWER: B

Basic rights and freedoms belonging to every human person

Explanation: Human rights belong to every person because they are human. They protect life, liberty, dignity, and fair treatment.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

They are not special rewards for selected people.

QUESTION 2ANSWER: D

Article III, Section 1

Explanation: This Bill of Rights provision protects life, liberty, and property from deprivation without due process of law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Due process means fair procedures and lawful government action.

QUESTION 3ANSWER: A

Equal treatment of persons in similar situations

Explanation: Equal protection requires similar cases to be treated alike. Reasonable legal classifications may still be allowed.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Equal protection means fairness, not always identical treatment.

QUESTION 4ANSWER: C

Commission on Human Rights (CHR)

Explanation: The CHR investigates human rights violations involving civil and political rights and promotes human rights protection. It is not a criminal court.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

CHR investigates and promotes; courts decide criminal guilt.

QUESTION 5ANSWER: B

Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures

Explanation: Article III, Section 2 protects people and their homes, papers, and belongings from unreasonable searches and seizures.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

A valid warrant is generally required, subject to recognized exceptions.

QUESTION 6ANSWER: D

Probable cause personally determined by a judge

Explanation: A judge must personally determine probable cause after examining the complainant and witnesses under oath. The warrant must particularly describe the place and items involved.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Remember: probable cause, judge, oath, particular description.

QUESTION 7ANSWER: A

Freedom of speech and expression

Explanation: Article III, Section 4 protects speech, expression, and the press, subject to valid legal limits.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

People may express ideas and opinions, within lawful limits.

QUESTION 8ANSWER: C

Right to peaceably assemble and petition the government

Explanation: Citizens may gather peacefully to express concerns and ask the government to act. This is protected by Article III, Section 4.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Peaceful protest and petition are constitutionally protected.

QUESTION 9ANSWER: B

Free exercise of religion; no government establishment of religion

Explanation: The Constitution protects religious belief and worship and prohibits the government from establishing a religion or preventing its free exercise.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Remember both: no establishment and free exercise.

QUESTION 10ANSWER: D

National security, public safety, or public health

Explanation: Article III, Section 6 allows restrictions on travel as provided by law for these three interests.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Memorize the three grounds: national security, public safety, public health.

QUESTION 11ANSWER: A

Right to information

Explanation: Article III, Section 7 protects access to information and official records on matters of public concern, subject to legal limitations.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Transparency is protected, but access is not unlimited.

QUESTION 12ANSWER: C

Freedom to organize for lawful purposes

Explanation: Article III, Section 8 protects the right to form unions, associations, or societies for purposes not contrary to law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

The purpose of the organization must be lawful.

QUESTION 13ANSWER: B

Just compensation

Explanation: Article III, Section 9 says private property cannot be taken for public use without just compensation.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Taking private property for public use requires fair payment.

QUESTION 14ANSWER: D

Free access to courts and adequate legal assistance

Explanation: Article III, Section 11 says poverty cannot be the reason a person is denied access to courts or adequate legal assistance.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Lack of money alone must not block access to justice.

QUESTION 15ANSWER: A

Right to remain silent and right to competent, independent counsel

Explanation: Article III, Section 12 requires authorities to inform a person under investigation of these rights. Counsel is preferably of the person's own choice.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

During custodial investigation: silence and counsel.

QUESTION 16ANSWER: C

In writing and in the presence of counsel

Explanation: A waiver of the rights under Article III, Section 12 is valid only when written and made in the presence of counsel.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Both requirements are needed: written AND with counsel.

QUESTION 17ANSWER: B

Torture, force, violence, threats, or intimidation

Explanation: Article III, Section 12 prohibits methods that destroy a person's free will during custodial investigation.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Statements must be voluntary; coercion is prohibited.

QUESTION 18ANSWER: D

Temporary liberty before conviction, subject to legal conditions

Explanation: Bail secures the release of a person in custody to ensure court appearance. The Constitution provides exceptions, including certain serious offenses when evidence of guilt is strong.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Bail does not erase a charge or guarantee acquittal.

QUESTION 19ANSWER: A

Right against self-incrimination

Explanation: Article III, Section 17 says no person can be compelled to be a witness against himself or herself.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

You cannot be forced to give testimonial evidence against yourself.

QUESTION 20ANSWER: C

Innocent until proven guilty according to law

Explanation: Article III, Section 14(2) presumes an accused person innocent unless guilt is proven according to law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

The prosecution must prove guilt; accusation alone is not proof.

QUESTION 21ANSWER: B

Protection against double jeopardy

Explanation: Article III, Section 21 protects a person from being tried or punished twice for the same offense when the constitutional requirements are met.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Double jeopardy prevents a second prosecution after jeopardy has legally attached and the case has ended in a qualifying way.

QUESTION 22ANSWER: D

To challenge the legality of a person's detention

Explanation: Habeas corpus asks the custodian to bring the detained person before the court and justify the detention.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Habeas corpus challenges unlawful detention or restraint.

QUESTION 23ANSWER: A

Prohibition against ex post facto laws

Explanation: Article III, Section 22 prohibits laws that retroactively criminalize an act or impose prohibited punishment for an earlier act.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

A new criminal law cannot punish conduct that was lawful when done.

QUESTION 24ANSWER: C

No imprisonment for debt or nonpayment of poll tax

Explanation: Article III, Section 20 prohibits imprisonment for debt or nonpayment of a poll tax. It does not automatically cancel the debt.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Debt alone is not a ground for imprisonment under this rule.

QUESTION 25ANSWER: B

CHR investigates civil and political human rights violations and promotes human rights protection

Explanation: The CHR investigates and promotes human rights. It does not convict accused persons or replace the courts.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

CHR investigates; courts determine criminal guilt and impose penalties.

Peace, Equality, and Human Dignity

Questions 1–25 · Answers, explanations, and quick lessons

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 1ANSWER: C

The dignity of every human person

Explanation: Article II, Section 11 of the 1987 Constitution states that the State values the dignity of every human person and guarantees full respect for human rights.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Human dignity is inherent. Every person deserves respect, whatever their status or wealth.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 2ANSWER: A

To protect life, liberty, property, and the general welfare

Explanation: Peaceful communities help protect people and their rights while supporting public safety and the common good.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Peace is not silence or fear; it is safety, respect, and protection of rights.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 3ANSWER: D

Every person has inherent worth and deserves respect

Explanation: Human dignity is the value every person has simply by being human. It is not earned through money, education, or position.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Dignity belongs to everyone; it is not something people must earn.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 4ANSWER: B

Everyone is entitled to equal protection under the law

Explanation: Equal protection means the law must treat people in similar situations fairly. Public position or wealth does not place someone above the law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

No one is above the law; everyone is entitled to equal protection.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 5ANSWER: C

RA 9710

Explanation: Republic Act No. 9710 is the Magna Carta of Women. It is a comprehensive law recognizing, protecting, and promoting women's rights.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Magna Carta of Women = RA 9710.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 6ANSWER: A

To promote women's empowerment and substantive equality

Explanation: The Magna Carta of Women aims to eliminate discrimination and advance women's rights, opportunities, and empowerment in all areas of life.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Substantive equality means addressing real barriers so women can enjoy rights and opportunities equally.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 7ANSWER: D

RA 7610

Explanation: Republic Act No. 7610 is the Special Protection of Children Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Child protection law in this question: RA 7610.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 8ANSWER: B

Equality and non-discrimination

Explanation: Treating students fairly regardless of economic background demonstrates that people should not be unfairly excluded or disadvantaged because of their status.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Non-discrimination means people should not face unfair treatment because of personal or social characteristics.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 9ANSWER: C

Dialogue, understanding, and peaceful resolution

Explanation: Dialogue allows people to explain their views, listen to others, and find solutions without threats or violence.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Resolve disagreements through communication and respect, not force.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 10ANSWER: A

Article II, Section 10

Explanation: Article II, Section 10 of the Constitution states that the State shall promote social justice in all phases of national development.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Social justice is specifically stated in Article II, Section 10.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 11ANSWER: D

Avoiding unfair treatment based on personal characteristics or identity

Explanation: Non-discrimination means people should not be treated unfairly because of characteristics such as sex, religion, disability, ethnicity, or social background.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Equal respect means avoiding unfair exclusion and prejudice.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 12ANSWER: B

Respecting different cultural practices and identities

Explanation: Respect for cultural diversity means recognizing and respecting different traditions, languages, and identities while upholding human rights.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Cultural differences are not a reason to exclude or mistreat people.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 13ANSWER: C

The youth

Explanation: Article II, Section 13 recognizes the vital role of the youth in nation-building and directs the State to promote and protect their physical, moral, spiritual, intellectual, and social well-being.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

The Constitution recognizes the youth as vital to nation-building.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 14ANSWER: A

Equal rights, opportunities, and treatment regardless of gender

Explanation: Gender equality means people should enjoy equal rights and opportunities without unfair disadvantage based on gender.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Equality is about equal rights and opportunities, not special superiority for one gender.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 15ANSWER: D

Providing reasonable access, inclusion, and equal opportunities

Explanation: Respecting the dignity of a person with a disability includes removing barriers, supporting accessibility, and ensuring meaningful participation.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Inclusion means access, participation, and equal opportunity—not exclusion or assumptions.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 16ANSWER: B

To investigate human rights violations involving civil and political rights and promote human rights protection

Explanation: The Commission on Human Rights investigates violations involving civil and political rights and promotes human rights. It does not approve criminal convictions.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

CHR investigates and promotes human rights; it is not a criminal court.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 17ANSWER: C

Torturing a person during an investigation

Explanation: Torture violates human dignity and constitutional protections. Peaceful demonstrations, equal education, and access to public information are rights-related activities, not violations by themselves.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Torture and coercive treatment are serious human rights violations.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 18ANSWER: A

Promoting fairness and addressing social, economic, and political inequalities

Explanation: Social justice seeks fair opportunities and treatment and addresses conditions that disadvantage people or groups.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Social justice works to reduce unfair inequalities and promote fairness.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 19ANSWER: D

It promotes fairness, trust, dignity, and peaceful coexistence

Explanation: Respecting human rights helps people trust institutions and one another. It reduces abuse and supports peaceful relationships in society.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Human rights support peace by protecting fairness, dignity, and trust.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 20ANSWER: B

Children deserve protection, care, development, and age-appropriate participation

Explanation: Children have rights to protection, survival, development, and participation. Their views should be considered in ways appropriate to their age and maturity.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Children are rights-holders and deserve protection, care, and opportunities to develop.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 21ANSWER: C

Women and men should enjoy fundamental equality before the law

Explanation: Article II, Section 14 recognizes the role of women in nation-building and requires the State to ensure the fundamental equality of women and men before the law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Article II, Section 14: women and men must enjoy fundamental equality before the law.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 22ANSWER: A

Resolving community conflicts through dialogue and respect

Explanation: Listening and discussing disagreements respectfully helps communities settle conflicts without discrimination, threats, or violence.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Dialogue and mutual respect help build peaceful communities.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 23ANSWER: D

All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights

Explanation: The Universal Declaration of Human Rights begins by affirming that all human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

UDHR Article 1: everyone is born free and equal in dignity and rights.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 25ANSWER: C

Peaceful coexistence and respect for human dignity

Explanation: A leader who listens to different groups and makes fair decisions demonstrates respect, inclusion, and peaceful coexistence.

Good community leadership listens fairly and respects every person's dignity.

Pollution and Waste Management

Questions 1–25 · Answers, explanations, and quick lessons

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 1ANSWER: C

To protect public health and the environment

Explanation: Proper waste management reduces pollution, prevents disease, conserves resources, and keeps communities clean and safe.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Manage waste properly to protect people, land, air, and water.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 2ANSWER: B

RA 9003

Explanation: Republic Act No. 9003 is the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000. It establishes a systematic approach to solid waste reduction, segregation, collection, and recovery.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Ecological Solid Waste Management Act = RA 9003.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 3ANSWER: D

Separating waste according to its type and characteristics

Explanation: Waste segregation means separating materials such as biodegradable, recyclable, residual, and special waste so each can be handled properly.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Segregate waste; do not mix everything in one bin.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 4ANSWER: A

Vegetable peelings

Explanation: Vegetable peelings are organic materials that microorganisms can break down naturally, so they are biodegradable.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Food scraps and plant materials are commonly biodegradable.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 5ANSWER: C

Composting

Explanation: Composting allows organic waste to decompose into a nutrient-rich, soil-like material that can improve soil.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Composting turns suitable organic waste into useful soil conditioner.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 6ANSWER: B

At the source, such as homes, schools, and businesses

Explanation: RA 9003 requires segregation at source. Separating waste where it is generated makes collection, recycling, and composting more effective.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Segregation begins where waste is produced: at home, school, or work.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 7ANSWER: D

Clean and dry aluminum cans

Explanation: Clean aluminum cans can be collected and processed into new materials. Food-contaminated tissue and organic scraps are not typical recyclable aluminum or packaging materials.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Keep recyclables clean and dry for recovery.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 8ANSWER: A

RA 8749

Explanation: Republic Act No. 8749 is the Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999, which provides a comprehensive air pollution control policy.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Clean Air Act = RA 8749.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 9ANSWER: C

To protect water quality and prevent water pollution

Explanation: The Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004, RA 9275, aims to protect the country's water bodies from pollution from land-based sources.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Clean Water Act = RA 9275; it protects water quality.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 10ANSWER: B

Discharging untreated industrial wastewater into a river

Explanation: Untreated industrial wastewater may contain chemicals and other contaminants that harm aquatic ecosystems and make water unsafe.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Discharging untreated wastewater into rivers causes water pollution.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 11ANSWER: D

Those responsible for pollution should bear the costs of preventing and addressing the damage

Explanation: The polluter-pays principle places the cost of pollution prevention, control, and remediation on those responsible for causing pollution.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Polluter pays: the party causing pollution bears the appropriate costs.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 12ANSWER: A

DENR leads national environmental management; implementation duties may be shared with LGUs and other agencies.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

MRF = Materials Recovery Facility for sorting and recovering useful materials.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 14ANSWER: B

Local government units

⚡ QUICK LESSON

LGUs have primary local responsibility for solid waste management under RA 9003.

Open burning of solid waste

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Open burning of solid waste is prohibited under RA 9003.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 16ANSWER: A

To process used materials into new products or materials

Explanation: Recycling processes recovered materials so they can be used to make new products, reducing demand for raw resources and the amount of waste disposed.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Recycling turns recovered materials into usable materials or products.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 17ANSWER: C

RA 6969

Explanation: Republic Act No. 6969 is the Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes Control Act of 1990.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Toxic Substances and Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes Control Act = RA 6969.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 18ANSWER: B

Certain discarded chemical substances that may harm human health or the environment

Explanation: Some discarded chemicals are hazardous because their properties can cause harm to people or the environment. They require proper handling and disposal.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Hazardous waste can harm health or the environment; never mix or dump it carelessly.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 19ANSWER: D

To remove or reduce contaminants before wastewater is discharged or reused

Explanation: Wastewater treatment removes or reduces pollutants so discharged or reused water causes less harm. Treatment does not automatically make water safe to drink.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Treat wastewater to reduce contaminants before discharge or reuse.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 20ANSWER: A

Releasing excessive smoke and harmful emissions from vehicles and factories

Explanation: Vehicle exhaust and industrial emissions can release pollutants into the air, contributing to poor air quality and health problems.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Control smoke and emissions from vehicles and industrial sources.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 21ANSWER: B

A sanitary landfill is an engineered disposal facility designed to control environmental risks

Explanation: A sanitary landfill is planned and engineered with measures to manage waste and reduce risks such as leachate and gas. An open dump lacks comparable controls.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Sanitary landfill = engineered controls; open dump = uncontrolled disposal.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 22ANSWER: C

Using reusable bags instead of repeatedly taking disposable plastic bags

Explanation: Using a reusable bag reduces the number of single-use bags consumed and discarded. Waste prevention is preferable to managing waste after it is created.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Reduce waste at the source by choosing reusable items.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 23ANSWER: D

Electronic or special waste

Explanation: Discarded televisions, radios, and computers are electronic waste, commonly called e-waste. They may contain materials that require special collection and handling.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Old electronics are e-waste; do not place them in ordinary mixed waste.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 24ANSWER: A

Preventing waste and reducing it should be prioritized before recycling, treatment, and disposal

Explanation: The waste hierarchy gives priority to preventing and reducing waste, followed by reuse and recycling or recovery, with treatment and final disposal lower in priority.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Best order: prevent and reduce first; disposal is the last resort.

POLLUTION & WASTE MANAGEMENT · QUESTION 25ANSWER: C

It encourages people and communities to help protect the environment through responsible practices

Explanation: Environmental programs work better when residents segregate waste, reduce litter, conserve resources, and follow local rules. Public participation complements government enforcement.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Environmental protection is a shared responsibility of government, communities, and individuals.
