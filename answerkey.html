CSC Mastery Answer Key

Fundamental Human Rights · Questions 1–25

Study tip: Read each explanation, then remember the key rule in its Quick Lesson.
QUESTION 1ANSWER: B

Basic rights and freedoms belonging to every human person

Explanation: Human rights belong to every person because they are human. They protect life, liberty, dignity, and fair treatment.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

They are not special rewards for selected people.

QUESTION 2ANSWER: D

Article III, Section 1

Explanation: This Bill of Rights provision protects life, liberty, and property from deprivation without due process of law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Due process means fair procedures and lawful government action.

QUESTION 3ANSWER: A

Equal treatment of persons in similar situations

Explanation: Equal protection requires similar cases to be treated alike. Reasonable legal classifications may still be allowed.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Equal protection means fairness, not always identical treatment.

QUESTION 4ANSWER: C

Commission on Human Rights (CHR)

Explanation: The CHR investigates human rights violations involving civil and political rights and promotes human rights protection. It is not a criminal court.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

CHR investigates and promotes; courts decide criminal guilt.

QUESTION 5ANSWER: B

Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures

Explanation: Article III, Section 2 protects people and their homes, papers, and belongings from unreasonable searches and seizures.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

A valid warrant is generally required, subject to recognized exceptions.

QUESTION 6ANSWER: D

Probable cause personally determined by a judge

Explanation: A judge must personally determine probable cause after examining the complainant and witnesses under oath. The warrant must particularly describe the place and items involved.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Remember: probable cause, judge, oath, particular description.

QUESTION 7ANSWER: A

Freedom of speech and expression

Explanation: Article III, Section 4 protects speech, expression, and the press, subject to valid legal limits.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

People may express ideas and opinions, within lawful limits.

QUESTION 8ANSWER: C

Right to peaceably assemble and petition the government

Explanation: Citizens may gather peacefully to express concerns and ask the government to act. This is protected by Article III, Section 4.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Peaceful protest and petition are constitutionally protected.

QUESTION 9ANSWER: B

Free exercise of religion; no government establishment of religion

Explanation: The Constitution protects religious belief and worship and prohibits the government from establishing a religion or preventing its free exercise.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Remember both: no establishment and free exercise.

QUESTION 10ANSWER: D

National security, public safety, or public health

Explanation: Article III, Section 6 allows restrictions on travel as provided by law for these three interests.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Memorize the three grounds: national security, public safety, public health.

QUESTION 11ANSWER: A

Right to information

Explanation: Article III, Section 7 protects access to information and official records on matters of public concern, subject to legal limitations.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Transparency is protected, but access is not unlimited.

QUESTION 12ANSWER: C

Freedom to organize for lawful purposes

Explanation: Article III, Section 8 protects the right to form unions, associations, or societies for purposes not contrary to law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

The purpose of the organization must be lawful.

QUESTION 13ANSWER: B

Just compensation

Explanation: Article III, Section 9 says private property cannot be taken for public use without just compensation.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Taking private property for public use requires fair payment.

QUESTION 14ANSWER: D

Free access to courts and adequate legal assistance

Explanation: Article III, Section 11 says poverty cannot be the reason a person is denied access to courts or adequate legal assistance.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Lack of money alone must not block access to justice.

QUESTION 15ANSWER: A

Right to remain silent and right to competent, independent counsel

Explanation: Article III, Section 12 requires authorities to inform a person under investigation of these rights. Counsel is preferably of the person's own choice.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

During custodial investigation: silence and counsel.

QUESTION 16ANSWER: C

In writing and in the presence of counsel

Explanation: A waiver of the rights under Article III, Section 12 is valid only when written and made in the presence of counsel.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Both requirements are needed: written AND with counsel.

QUESTION 17ANSWER: B

Torture, force, violence, threats, or intimidation

Explanation: Article III, Section 12 prohibits methods that destroy a person's free will during custodial investigation.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Statements must be voluntary; coercion is prohibited.

QUESTION 18ANSWER: D

Temporary liberty before conviction, subject to legal conditions

Explanation: Bail secures the release of a person in custody to ensure court appearance. The Constitution provides exceptions, including certain serious offenses when evidence of guilt is strong.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Bail does not erase a charge or guarantee acquittal.

QUESTION 19ANSWER: A

Right against self-incrimination

Explanation: Article III, Section 17 says no person can be compelled to be a witness against himself or herself.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

You cannot be forced to give testimonial evidence against yourself.

QUESTION 20ANSWER: C

Innocent until proven guilty according to law

Explanation: Article III, Section 14(2) presumes an accused person innocent unless guilt is proven according to law.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

The prosecution must prove guilt; accusation alone is not proof.

QUESTION 21ANSWER: B

Protection against double jeopardy

Explanation: Article III, Section 21 protects a person from being tried or punished twice for the same offense when the constitutional requirements are met.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Double jeopardy prevents a second prosecution after jeopardy has legally attached and the case has ended in a qualifying way.

QUESTION 22ANSWER: D

To challenge the legality of a person's detention

Explanation: Habeas corpus asks the custodian to bring the detained person before the court and justify the detention.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Habeas corpus challenges unlawful detention or restraint.

QUESTION 23ANSWER: A

Prohibition against ex post facto laws

Explanation: Article III, Section 22 prohibits laws that retroactively criminalize an act or impose prohibited punishment for an earlier act.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

A new criminal law cannot punish conduct that was lawful when done.

QUESTION 24ANSWER: C

No imprisonment for debt or nonpayment of poll tax

Explanation: Article III, Section 20 prohibits imprisonment for debt or nonpayment of a poll tax. It does not automatically cancel the debt.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

Debt alone is not a ground for imprisonment under this rule.

QUESTION 25ANSWER: B

CHR investigates civil and political human rights violations and promotes human rights protection

Explanation: The CHR investigates and promotes human rights. It does not convict accused persons or replace the courts.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

CHR investigates; courts determine criminal guilt and impose penalties.

The youth

The Constitution recognizes the youth as vital to nation-building.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 14ANSWER: A

Equal rights, opportunities, and treatment regardless of gender

Equality is about equal rights and opportunities, not special superiority for one gender.

PEACE, EQUALITY & HUMAN DIGNITY · QUESTION 16ANSWER: B

To investigate human rights violations involving civil and political rights and promote human rights protection

Explanation: The Commission on Human Rights investigates violations involving civil and political rights and promotes human rights. It does not approve criminal convictions.

⚡ QUICK LESSON

CHR investigates and promotes human rights; it is not a criminal court.
